# Release Notes: 1 November 2026

## 1. Grammars Written in BNF, and the Diagnosis of a Rejected Text

In this update morphe reads a grammar written as text and parses with it ([decision 0009](../decisions/0009-grammars-written-in-bnf.md); the infrastructure's [ADR 0064](https://github.com/huangwanhong-maker/The-Science-Infrastructures/blob/main/development/decisions/0064-abnormal-genomes-their-description-and-expression.md)).

- **BNF over bytes.** A grammar is written as a standard writes it: literals, classes of bytes, groups, `?`, `*`, `+`, and hidden rules. It is compiled to a context-free grammar over the fewest terminals that tell its classes apart, and Earley's parser reads a named tree back. Errors in the grammar's own text are reported by line and column, among them rules that derive no word.
- **Exact diagnoses.** Earley's parser now reports the furthest viable prefix of a word and the letters that may follow it. A rejected text is therefore told exactly how far it is right and what it lacks. For example: *at column 3, after "du": unexpected 'x'; expected 'p' (of 'dup')*.
- **Checked against references.** 80 random regular grammars agree with `std::regex` on every short word. On languages enumerated in full, the diagnoses are exact.
- **First use.** The genomics domain writes HGVS's and ISCN's grammars, the international notations of variants and chromosomes, in this notation.
