# Release Notes: 29 October 2026

## 1. Representations of Finite Groups, and Grammars with Symmetry

In this update morphe computes with finite groups' representations exactly, parses grammars, and parses them up to a group's symmetry ([decision 0006](../decisions/0006-representations-and-grammars-with-symmetry.md)). The infrastructure's genomics domain uses these to make a grammar of genomes built on a group exact.

- **Representations, exactly.** One can now:
  - compute in cyclotomic fields;
  - find the character table of any finite group (Dixon–Schneider), with values in those fields;
  - split a group algebra into its Wedderburn blocks;
  - build the irreducible representations of the symmetric groups in Young's seminormal form;
  - take Fourier transforms on S_n, on abelian groups and on the Walsh–Hadamard cube.
- **Group actions.** A group acts on an alphabet with a sign, so a reversal is allowed. Orbits, stabilisers and Burnside's counts of words follow.
- **Grammars.** Earley's parser and inside–outside run over any semiring, and a search decides a monotone grammar.
- **Grammars with symmetry.**
  - A grammar that a group leaves invariant has its rules counted in orbits.
  - It is parsed by an equivariant Earley parser that binds each rule's group element as a coset.
  - Its chart stands exactly for the plain one, at about half its size.
  - Spans are shared across orbits, and the orbit-canonical search, breakings and coarse-to-fine masks are exact.
  - The checks show the draft's per-position orbit scan accepting words outside the language.
- **Repetitive collections.** The r-index and the positional BWT index collections of similar texts, and increasing subsequences are found in O(n log n).
- **Dependency.** `formal` now uses `algebra`.

## 2. Graph Rewriting

In this update morphe rewrites graphs. A rule is a double pushout: what it needs, what it keeps and what it makes. A kept vertex or edge may change its label (Habel and Plump's relabelling), so an atom can change its charge and keep its identity.

morphe finds every match of a rule in a graph and every derivation, and explores what a start graph derives, up to isomorphism. It counts the derivations of every length that reach each class, and the orbits of the classes under permutations of the labels. It also tests whether derivations commute with such a permutation, and whether the rule set is closed under it, the hypothesis that makes them commute.

Counting is exact: a rule with symmetries matches |Aut L| times each place it occurs. Classes of graphs made of many identical parts stay exact, each component encoded on its own.

The infrastructure's Chemistry Workbench runs a manuscript's chemical grammars on it ([decision 0007](../decisions/0007-graph-rewriting.md)).
