# algebra: Exact Linear Algebra, Homology, Groups and Their Representations

*Status: draft (2026-10-24): integer matrices and the Smith normal form, chain complexes and their homology, finitely presented groups, finite groups; linear algebra over exact fields, character tables, group algebras and their Wedderburn decomposition, the representations of the symmetric groups, Fourier transforms on finite groups.* Hermite normal forms, permutation groups by Schreier–Sims, coset enumeration, rewriting, Lie algebras over exact rings and universal algebra are planned ([decision 0004](../development/decisions/0004-the-first-slice-cell-complexes.md), §2.1).

*New (2026-10-24):* the representation theory of finite groups, exactly: the character table of any group given by its table (Dixon–Schneider, values in ℚ(ζ_e)); the group algebra K[G] with its central primitive idempotents and its blocks, ℂ[G] ≅ ⊕_χ M_{χ(1)}(ℂ) measured rather than assumed, and the rational blocks, ℚ[C_n] ≅ ⊕_{d | n} ℚ(ζ_d); Young's seminormal form of S_n with the Murnaghan–Nakayama rule and the hook length formula, the Fourier transform on S_n and the matrix units of ℚ[S_n]; the Fourier transform of finite abelian groups and the fast Walsh–Hadamard transform. They are the algebra of group-equivariant grammars for genomes.

## 1. Purpose

The algebra that topology, physics and the study of symmetric structures need exactly: linear algebra over ℤ and over exact fields, the homology of chain complexes with its generators, groups given by generators and relations, finite groups with the homomorphisms into them, and their representations: characters, group algebras and Fourier transforms. Its numerical counterpart, linear algebra in floating point, is calx's `linalg`; Lie groups acting on space are physis's `symmetry`.

## 2. Concepts

- **The Smith normal form** U A V = diag(d₁ | d₂ | … | d_r, 0, …) with U, V unimodular (Smith 1861; Newman 1972, Theorem II.9). The invariant factors give the abelian group a matrix presents, and the rank over ℚ. The algorithm brings a least entry to the pivot, clears its row and column by division with remainder, and restores divisibility (Cohen 1993, Algorithm 2.4.14); the transforms and their inverses are accumulated on request.
- **Homology in two phases.** First an algebraic reduction of the chain complex by unit pivots (Kaczynski, Mischaikow and Mrozek 2004, §4.4), which for a cell complex contracts a spanning tree and removes free faces, keeping for each surviving cell its lift to an original chain; then the Smith normal form of the small remainder, from whose transforms the generators are read (Munkres 1984, §11). Generators are cycles of the original complex: a basis of the free part, and a cycle of the stated order for each torsion factor. Cohomology follows by the universal coefficient theorem, Betti numbers over 𝔽_p from ranks modulo p.
- **Presentations and Tietze moves.** Words are sequences of letters ±i; `simplify` reduces relators freely and cyclically, removes repeats up to rotation and inversion, and eliminates a generator occurring once in a relator (Lyndon and Schupp 1977, §II.2). It terminates and preserves the group, and claims no normal form: the isomorphism problem is undecidable (Adian 1957; Rabin 1958).
- **Finite groups and homomorphisms.** Groups by their tables, built for the classical families and by closure of permutations; homomorphisms from a presented group are enumerated by backtracking, testing each relator as soon as its generators are assigned. For the fundamental group of a surface the count is a character sum (Frobenius 1896; Mednykh 1978), which the checks use.
- **Linear algebra over a field.** Gaussian elimination is written once for any exact field K (ℚ, ℚ(ζ_m)): rank, determinant and inverse in O(m n r) operations for an m × n matrix of rank r. Over ℚ it is held to the Smith form's ranks and to Bareiss's determinants over ℤ.
- **Characters by the Dixon–Schneider method.** *Definitions.* For the classes K_j (h_j = |K_j|, g_j ∈ K_j) the class sums C_j span the centre of ℂ[G], C_j C_k = Σ_l a_{jkl} C_l with a_{jkl} = #{(x, y) ∈ K_j × K_k : x y = g_l}; the central character of an irreducible χ is ω_χ(C_j) = h_j χ(g_j)/χ(1). *Result.* ω_χ is an algebra homomorphism of the centre, so w_χ = (ω_χ(C_j))_j is a common eigenvector of the class matrices M_j = (a_{jkl})_{kl}, and the r vectors w_χ are, up to scalars, all of them. *The algorithm* (Dixon 1967; Schneider 1990): compute the common eigenvectors modulo the least prime p ≡ 1 (mod e) with p > 2√|G|. The reductions stay independent, since the column orthogonality relations survive modulo p with the units |C_G(g)| on the right, so the common eigenspaces are lines; they are found one class matrix at a time, each space split by the eigenspaces of the next matrix restricted to it. Then χ(1)² ≡ |G| / Σ_j ω_j ω_{j*} / h_j has one root in [1, √|G|]; the values θ(χ(g)) follow; and each is lifted to ℤ[ζ_e] exactly through the multiplicities m_k = (1/o) Σ_i χ(g^i) ζ_o^{−ik} of the eigenvalues of ρ(g), integers in [0, χ(1)] below p. It terminates on every finite group, in O(|G|² + |G| r + r⁴ + p r²) operations. The table carries the classes, their sizes and representatives, the class of each element and the inverse classes; with it come power maps, inner products of class functions, Frobenius–Schur indicators (Isaacs 1976, ch. 4) and the Galois conjugates of characters.
- **The group algebra and its blocks.** K[G] has the elements as basis and the convolution product. *Results* (Serre 1977, §2.6, Theorem 8, and ch. 6): e_χ = (χ(1)/|G|) Σ_g χ(g⁻¹) g are the central primitive idempotents of ℂ[G], e_χ e_ψ = δ_{χψ} e_χ, Σ_χ e_χ = 1, and ℂ[G] e_χ ≅ M_{χ(1)}(ℂ), so ℂ[G] ≅ ⊕_χ M_{χ(1)}(ℂ). Nothing of this is assumed in the code: the idempotents are formed from the computed table, their relations checked by exact multiplication, and each block's dimension measured as the rank of x ↦ x e_χ. Any f is the sum of its isotypic components f e_χ. Over ℚ the central primitive idempotents are the sums of the e_χ over the Galois orbits of characters (proved in `group_algebra.hpp`), of dimension |orbit| χ(1)², and there are as many as conjugacy classes of cyclic subgroups (Serre 1977, §13.1), which the checks count independently. For C_n this is ℚ[C_n] ≅ ⊕_{d | n} ℚ(ζ_d), each block generated by a root of Φ_d.
- **The representations of S_n.** Young's seminormal form gives ρ_λ by rational matrices for the Coxeter generators (i i+1) on the standard tableaux of shape λ: its diagonal the reciprocal axial distances, forced by the Jucys–Murphy elements, its off-diagonal pairs (1, 1 − α²) a rescaling of Young's orthogonal form (Okounkov and Vershik 1996); ρ(σ) is the product along the bubble-sort word of σ. The Murnaghan–Nakayama rule on β-numbers gives the characters as integers (Sagan 2001, §4.10), the hook length formula the degrees (Frame, Robinson and Thrall 1954; Sagan 2001, §3.10). *The Fourier transform* (Diaconis 1988, ch. 2): f̂(λ) = Σ_σ f(σ) ρ_λ(σ), inverted by f(σ) = (1/n!) Σ_λ d_λ tr(ρ_λ(σ⁻¹) f̂(λ)), with (f ∗ g)^ = f̂ ĝ for the product of ℚ[S_n]. The inverse transforms E^λ_{ST} of the matrix units are matrix units of ℚ[S_n], the explicit isomorphism ℚ[S_n] ≅ ⊕_λ M_{d_λ}(ℚ); the E^λ_{TT} are primitive idempotents generating minimal left ideals, and sum over T to e_λ.
- **Fourier transforms of abelian groups.** For A = C_{n_1} × ⋯ × C_{n_k}, f̂(y) = Σ_x f(x) ζ_N^{⟨x, y⟩} in ℚ(ζ_N), N = lcm(n_i), computed axis by axis in O(|A| Σ n_i), with its inverse, the convolution theorem and Plancherel's identity; for (C_2)^k, the Walsh–Hadamard transform over ℤ by k passes of butterflies, O(k 2^k), its matrix H_2^{⊗k} (Sylvester 1867), the inverse exact over ℚ.

## 7. Open Questions

1. **Large residues.** The dense phase is cubic; complexes whose reduction leaves thousands of cells would need modular Smith forms (Dumas, Saunders and Villard 2001).
2. **Larger groups.** The character table is computed from a multiplication table, so its |G|² entries must fit in memory, and every class matrix is formed in full. Groups of permutations by Schreier–Sims, class matrices formed from the elements column by column, and the Galois action used to split several eigenspaces at once would reach much larger groups. The formula of Frobenius and Mednykh and the gauge theories of finite groups can now use the computed tables.
3. **Coset enumeration and rewriting** (Todd–Coxeter, Knuth–Bendix) for presented groups that are finite or automatic.
4. **Representations beyond S_n.** Characters give the central idempotents of any group, but primitive (non-central) idempotents and explicit matrices need the representations themselves, and over ℚ their Schur indices as well; constructing them from the characters, or by induction from subgroups, is open.
5. **Fast Fourier transforms on S_n.** The transform here costs O((n!)²); transforms adapted to the chain S_1 ⊂ S_2 ⊂ ⋯ ⊂ S_n, for which the seminormal basis is made, would reduce it to a small power of n times n!.
